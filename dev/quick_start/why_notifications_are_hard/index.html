    Why Notifications Are Hard in Home Assistant¶

    This page explains why one message to several channels is harder than it looks in Home Assistant. It is the reason Supernotify exists, and it is also meant as a contribution to the current Home Assistant work on making notifications easier. It describes Home Assistant 2026.11, checked against its source code. Each release moves more integrations to notify entities, so some rows of the table will change.

    "Why can't I just use a group?"¶

    Say you want one alert to reach your phone, your e-mail, a family Telegram chat and an SMS to someone without a smartphone. Home Assistant has notify groups, so it seems natural to make a group of those four and send to it. There are two kinds of notify group, and neither can hold all four.

    The Notify group helper, set up in the UI, groups notify entities and sends with notify.send_message. The Companion App, SMTP and Telegram bot all have notify entities, so three of the four can go in. SMS can't: the SMS integrations, such as Twilio SMS and ClickSend, only have the older style of notify action. The group sends only a message and a title, and it drops the title for everyone if any member doesn't support titles. There is no way to add a camera snapshot, a button on the phone notification, an HTML e-mail body or anything else.

    The legacy notify group, YAML only, groups the older notify actions, like notify.mobile_app_annas_phone or notify.smtp. Telegram can't go in, because its notify action was removed in 2026.11 and only the notify entity is left. The other three can, but every member gets the same call:

    • target means something different to each one: e-mail addresses to SMTP and phone numbers to Twilio, while a phone's own action ignores it. One target can't be right for all of them.
    • data is sent to every member whatever its keys. A picture is data.image for the Companion App, data.images (a list of local files) for SMTP and data.media_url for Twilio SMS. Each member gets the keys meant for the others as well as its own.
    • Each member can have fixed data set in the YAML, but not data that changes per notification, such as an image for this alert.

    Neither kind of group knows who it is notifying. When a phone is replaced, the group has to be edited. If someone is away, or the message should only go to whoever is at home, the group can't decide that. Groups are just fixed lists of where to send.

    Why it's like this¶

    None of this is anyone's mistake. Home Assistant has added hundreds of notification integrations over ten years, each with the needs of its own service, and the shared part of notifications has always been very small:

    • Only message is common. title is common to most actions. target exists in one style and not in the others, and means something different in each integration. Everything else (images, buttons, priority, sound, HTML, attachments, tap actions) goes under a free-form data key in the legacy style, or into fields of an integration's own action in the newer style. No shared schema covers them.
    • The same idea has different names. A tap action is data.url on iOS but data.clickAction on Android, both in the Companion App. It is click in ntfy and url in Prowl and Pushover. Priority is -2 to 2 in Pushover and Prowl, 1 to 5 in ntfy, info/warning/critical in LaMetric, and interruption-level under push for the iOS Companion App.
    • Some services have an action per target. The legacy Companion App style makes a notify.mobile_app_<device> action for every phone, so the action name is the target. Automations name the phone, not the person, and have to be changed when the phone is replaced.
    • Some services have two styles at once. Newer integrations offer a notify entity, which works with notify.send_message, areas, labels and groups but sends only message and title. For everything else, they add an action of their own that is aimed at the same entity, such as smtp.send_message or ntfy.publish. To use an image, you have to leave the shared action for the integration's own action.
    • Speech, chimes and on-screen messages are separate again. tts.speak, persistent_notification.create, alexa_devices.send_sound and mqtt.publish each have their own shape and targets, and none of them is a notify action.

    Notify entities are the right direction: they can be targeted by area, label and device, and they show up properly in the UI. They don't yet carry anything beyond message and title, though, and many integrations, especially SMS, haven't moved to them.

    The styles of notification action¶

    1. Legacy notify action for a service¶

    One action for each configured service, with a shared shape: message, title, target and a free-form data. The meaning of target and the keys in data belong to each integration.

    action: notify.smtp
data:
  title: Front door
  message: The front door has been open for 10 minutes
  target:
    - anna@example.com
  data:
    images:
      - /config/www/snapshots/front_door.jpg

    The same action for Twilio SMS takes phone numbers as target and media_url in data.

    2. Legacy notify action for a device¶

    Same shape as style 1, but the integration makes one action per device, so the target is in the action name. The Companion App is the main example, with notify.mobile_app_<device> for each phone, and different data keys for iOS and Android.

    action: notify.mobile_app_annas_phone
data:
  title: Front door
  message: The front door has been open for 10 minutes
  data:
    image: /api/camera_proxy/camera.front_door
    url: /lovelace/doors          # iOS
    clickAction: /lovelace/doors  # Android
    actions:
      - action: CLOSE_DOOR
        title: Close it

    3. Notify entity with notify.send_message¶

    One shared action for every notify entity, which can be targeted by entity, device, area or label, and is fully supported in the UI editor. It takes only message and title, and the title is used only by entities that declare support for it. There is no data.

    action: notify.send_message
target:
  entity_id: notify.annas_phone
data:
  title: Front door
  message: The front door has been open for 10 minutes

    4. The integration's own action, aimed at its notify entity¶

    The integration's notify entity is used for the target, and the integration adds its own action with flat fields for everything notify.send_message can't send. The fields differ for each integration.

    action: ntfy.publish
target:
  entity_id: notify.ntfy_alerts
data:
  title: Front door
  message: The front door has been open for 10 minutes
  priority: 4
  click: https://my.home/lovelace/doors
  attach: https://my.home/local/snapshots/front_door.jpg

    smtp.send_message, html5.send_message, prowl.send_message, nfandroidtv.send_message and the telegram_bot.send_* actions work the same way, each with different fields.

    5. The integration's own action, with its own targets¶

    Actions that aren't based on notify at all, and pick their target their own way: a device, a room, a topic, or a media player alongside a TTS engine.

    action: tts.speak
target:
  entity_id: tts.home_assistant_cloud
data:
  media_player_entity_id: media_player.kitchen
  message: The front door has been open for 10 minutes

    Others are lametric.message (by device_id), alexa_devices.send_sound (by device_id), matrix.send_message (by room), mqtt.publish (by topic) and persistent_notification.create (no target, it appears in the Home Assistant UI).

    6. Groups¶

    The two kinds of notify group are described above. The Notify group helper is a notify entity used with style 3. The legacy notify group is a style 1 action that calls other style 1 or 2 actions.

    Integrations and their styles¶

    Home Assistant 2026.11. Both means the integration has two styles, where the notify entity is the easy one and the other is needed for anything beyond message and title.

    Telegram bot Removed in 2026.11 Yes, per chat telegram_bot.send_message, send_photo, ... (4) Own actions: keyboard, inline_keyboard, parse_mode, separate actions for photos and files

    Notifying a person, not a device¶

    Home Assistant knows who each person is and which device trackers follow them, but not how to reach them. The Home Assistant team is working on this, after the community "What the Heck?" discussion Notifying people is hard, with a proposal in architecture discussion #1552: each person gets a list of their notify entities, and notify.send_message can be sent to person.anna. That fixes the biggest everyday problem, replacing the phone in every automation, and makes notify entities the natural way to reach someone.

    Supernotify will support this as soon as possible once it is released, both by sending to a person through their linked notify entities and by letting notify.send_message to a person go through Supernotify's own routing.

    What Supernotify does about it¶

    Supernotify takes one notification, with one set of options, and turns it into the right call for each channel:

    • One action for everything. supernotify.notify takes the message, title, images, buttons and priority once, and each transport maps what its channel supports onto the style, field names and targets its integration needs, whether legacy action, notify entity, own action or TTS.
    • People, not devices. Recipients hold each person's e-mail, phone number, Telegram chat and so on, and their Companion App phones are found automatically. Notify person.anna and each channel gets the right address. Each recipient also gets its own notify entity.
    • Who should get it. Notifications can go only to people at home, or only to those away, and fall back to other channels or people. See the presence routing with fallback recipe.
    • What it's about. Scenarios and priorities decide which channels to use, so a low priority message can go only to the phone while a critical one also sounds chimes and speaks on speakers.
    • Not too often. Snoozing and duplicate detection stop the same alert repeating.
